# Provider stack on the shared result tuple

Migrate `ProviderResult<T>` and the provider surfaces onto
`Result<T, ProviderError>` per decisions/34-errors-as-values.md: the
`ChatProvider` interface (src/domain/providers/provider.ts), the ollama and
openai adapters (including the streaming chat generator's failure delivery
— design the tuple shape for async iteration deliberately and document it),
capability probing, the client factory, and their callers (chat turn
engine's ModelGateway port, selection store's model loading, options
test-connection). Known wire failures (401/403/404/429, unreachable,
malformed stream) are values; only invariant violations still throw.

## Comments

- **claude** (2026-08-22T14:20:00.000Z): **`ProviderResult<T>` is gone, and nothing replaced it.** src/domain/providers/provider.ts:179-186 is now a comment where the alias used to be, saying why there is no `ProviderResult` any more rather than quietly redefining it as `Result<T, ProviderError>`: decisions/34 asks for ONE shape, and a per-context alias over it is a second name the call sites then have to translate between. Spelling the vocabulary out at each signature is also what makes the MIXED sites readable — `ProvidersSection.svelte` holds a `StorageError` from the registry and a `ProviderError` from the client inside the same function, and now says which is which. `ChatProvider` reads `Promise<Result<ProviderModel[], ProviderError>>` and `Promise<Result<ModelCapabilities, ProviderError>>` (src/domain/providers/provider.ts:358-363); `resolveCapability` collapsed to one line at src/domain/providers/capability.ts:49-50, and its "a failed lookup folds into `unknown`, never into a guess" rule is unchanged. One narrowing beyond the mechanical swap: src/infra/ollama/client.ts declares `Result<T, OllamaError>` (client.ts:227, :339, :392) rather than the wider `ProviderError` the old alias carried. `OllamaError` was already the module's own `Extract<>` of the four kinds Ollama can produce; `Result` is a union of READONLY tuples and therefore covariant in its error member, so it still assigns straight into the interface's `Result<T, ProviderError>` with nothing to map at the boundary (src/infra/ollama/adapter.ts:153-159 passes a failure through untouched). What the narrower type buys is inside the folder: an exhaustive switch on an Ollama failure has four arms, not six, and a future `ProviderError` member for some other provider cannot silently make it non-exhaustive. src/infra/ollama/client.ts:95-112 documents that, and src/infra/ollama/client.test.ts:576-588 pins BOTH directions of it — the widening assignment compiles, and a `"auth"` failure in an `OllamaError` slot is a `@ts-expect-error`.

- **claude** (2026-08-22T14:20:00.000Z): **THE STREAMING SHAPE — the question card 92 flagged as genuinely open, settled and written down at src/domain/providers/provider.ts:286-322.** Card 92's guidance was to keep the event and give it a typed payload rather than wrap the iterable, and that is what landed, but the file now records the alternatives so the next reader does not re-derive them. Three shapes were on the table. (1) `AsyncGenerator<Result<ChatStreamEvent, ProviderError>>` — every consumer unwraps a tuple on every content delta, thousands of times a turn, to express something that happens at most once. (2) `Promise<Result<AsyncGenerator<…>, ProviderError>>` — puts SETUP failures (unreachable, 401, 404) in the return type but leaves MID-STREAM ones with nowhere to go, so one vocabulary would arrive down two different paths. (3) the terminal `{type:"error", error: ProviderError}` event, generator returns. (3) wins on the property that actually matters here: a stream has already produced OUTPUT by the time most of its failures happen, and (3) is the only one of the three where the tokens emitted before the fault stay in the transcript — a truncated reply plus a stated reason, instead of a discarded turn. The error is typed and exhaustively switchable exactly like a `Result` arm either way. **The invariant a consumer may rely on is now stated explicitly: `{type:"error"}` is terminal, at most one is emitted, nothing follows it.** src/domain/chat/ports.ts:34-47 says the same at the `ModelGateway` seam and points at the full rationale; src/domain/chat/turn.ts:367-379 needed NO change — it already reads the event into a `terminalError: ProviderError`, so the same exhaustive switch a one-shot caller writes works over a stream failure, and its `try` around the `for await` stays as belt-and-braces against a NON-compliant client, which the comment already said and still says.

- **claude** (2026-08-22T14:20:00.000Z): **Known wire failures are data; the concrete list is now written at each generator.** src/infra/ollama/client.ts:616-651 enumerates the five things that arrive as an event rather than a throw: a `fetch` rejection (dead server or blocked preflight — indistinguishable, and named as such), a non-2xx status (403 routed through `originRejectedError`, card 33), a 2xx with no body, a malformed NDJSON line or aborted read mid-stream, and a connection that closed without `done:true` — card 90's truncation signal. src/infra/openai/index.ts:510-532 does the same for SSE and, more usefully, records the two mid-stream faults that are deliberately NOT errors there because they are recoverable: a single malformed `data:` payload is skipped, and a close without the `[DONE]` sentinel finalizes normally. That is the opposite of Ollama's choice and the doc now says why it is not an inconsistency — OpenAI's last content event is complete on its own, whereas Ollama's `done:true` is the ONLY completion signal it has, so its absence can only be read as truncation. Both module headers also now state the catch discipline explicitly (src/infra/ollama/client.ts:19-37, src/infra/openai/index.ts:24-32): every `catch` in either adapter sits directly on a `fetch`, a `reader.read()`, a `response.json()`, or a `JSON.parse` of one wire payload — the fetch boundary and nothing above it.
